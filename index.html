        How a message gets home

        A suit radio cannot reach Earth. Every message hops through the habitat and a relay orbiter before a Deep Space Network dish picks it up.

        Equipment checklist

          How a suited body moves

          Body, suit and leg strength

          Model and assumptions
          • Metabolic power: Pandolf et al. (1977) load-carriage equation, M = 1.5W + 2.0(W+L)(L/W)² + η(W+L)(1.5V² + 0.35VG). The load and walking terms are scaled by g/9.81 and multiplied by the suit mobility factor (pressurised joints resist bending). Downhill grades are counted as flat, a conservative simplification.
          • Preferred speed: the slowest of (a) the speed that minimises Pandolf energy per metre, (b) the pendulum limit at Froude number 0.25, and (c) the fastest pace that keeps metabolic power under your sustainable effort. More mass raises the power needed, so (c) drops.
          • Walk-to-run switch: Froude number 0.5, which held in reduced-gravity tests (Kram, Domingo and Ferris, J. Exp. Biol. 1997).
          • Stride: Alexander's λ = 2.3·h·Fr0.3 at the chosen speed, shortened by 10% per added body mass of load (assumption, load studies show shorter strides).
          • Jump: force-limited. The legs push with a fixed force F (a capability, not tied to the mass you enter) over a push-off distance d = 0.38 m; take-off speed v = √(2(F − Mg)d / M), height = v²/2g. Heavier total mass M means a lower jump.
          • Fatigue index: metabolic energy spent so far on the planned walk as a share of the whole walk's energy.

          Design the habitat

          Engineering assumptions
          • Each module is a hemispherical dome. Shell volume = ⅔π((r+t)³ − r³).
          • Tunnels are 2.4 m wide pressurised cylinders with the same regolith cover as the domes.
          • Sintering heats basalt-like soil to about 1,100 °C; the energy per kilogram is an editable assumption.
          • Mars's atmosphere already gives about 20 g/cm² overhead. Galactic cosmic rays cannot be stopped completely, so a thicker shell gives diminishing returns.
          • Crew capacity uses a planning rule of thumb of 25 m³ pressurised volume per person for long stays.
          • Jezero has no confirmed near-surface ice, so water would come from hydrated minerals or be shipped in.
          • Reference: NASA 3D-Printed Habitat Challenge.

          How the walk sounds on Mars

          Mars air is 95% carbon dioxide at under 1% of Earth's pressure. Perseverance's SuperCam microphone measured the result: sound travels at about 240 m/s, high pitches arrive slightly faster than low ones, and they fade within a few metres. Pick a sound and a distance and hear the difference.

          Speeds: about 240 m/s below 240 Hz and 250 m/s above, measured with SuperCam's microphone (Maurice et al., Nature, 2022). The thin air makes sound about 20 dB weaker. The high-frequency fade here is a simplified curve tuned to the team's finding that two people 5 m apart would struggle to talk. Sounds are synthesised in your browser. Hear NASA's real recordings.
